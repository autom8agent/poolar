# Rules, venues and other people

Short, but worth reading before you take this out.

## It is not legal for sanctioned play

WPA, BCA and WPBSA equipment rules all require the cue to be a cue — no
electronics, no recording devices, no training aids attached during play. So:

- **practice, coaching, league-with-agreement, content — fine**
- **sanctioned or ranked matches — no**

This is exactly why the blank slug is a first-class part. Blank in, and the cue
is mechanically a plain carbon cue with a decorative collar, at the same weight
and the same balance you practised with. That is the honest version of "nothing
to declare", and it is also the only version that does not ambush you at a
tournament desk.

If you play in a league, ask the organiser once, in advance, and take the answer.

## Venues

Most rooms have a view about filming, and it is usually about their other
customers rather than about you. Ask the manager. It takes thirty seconds and it
means nobody is walking over to you mid-frame.

A cue-mounted camera is also less obvious than a tripod, which cuts both ways:
people are less bothered by it, and people also do not know it is there.

## Other people in frame

A 150° lens pointed down a table picks up everyone around it. You do not need to
turn this into a legal project, but a few defaults make it a non-issue:

- **Keep it to your own sessions**, not left running through someone else's
- If you post footage, look at who else is in it first
- Delete the card regularly — 40 minute recordings of a pub are not something
  you want an archive of
- If someone asks, show them. Most curiosity evaporates the moment you hand
  someone the cue and let them look at the lens.

In the UK, purely personal and household use sits outside UKGDPR — but that
exemption evaporates the moment you publish. Same thing in most of the EU. If
this becomes a channel rather than a practice tool, that is the point to think
about it properly.

## Battery, in public

A lithium pouch cell inside a sealed carbon tube, in your hands, near your face.
It is fine — millions of devices do exactly this — but it is fine *because* of
choices you have to actually make:

- protected cell only
- never charge it inside the cue
- bin it if the pouch has swelled at all
- check it after any drop

That is the entire safety story for this project, and it is worth the paragraph.
